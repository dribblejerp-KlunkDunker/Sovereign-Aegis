# Dark Triad Behavior Recognition — Exploitative Patterns, Boundaries, Documentation, Support

**Status:** reference guide (third lens guide alongside
[`mbti-comparison-lens.md`](mbti-comparison-lens.md) and
[`enneagram-comparison-lens.md`](enneagram-comparison-lens.md); implements the Dark Triad atlas
card in `DESIGN-personal-defense-profile.md` §7).
**Evidence tier:** `moderate` for the trait constructs behind the name (real research measures),
but this guide uses **none of them on people**. It uses only the behavior-recognition side: what
exploitative patterns look like from the outside, how to hold a boundary against them, how to
document your own experience, and where human support lives.

---

## Read this first — the three rules

1. **Name the tactic, not the person.** "Guilt followed by urgency" is an observable behavior you
   can act on. "They are a narcissist" is a verdict that forecloses evidence and that you are not
   qualified to make — no checklist here, and no personality test anywhere, can diagnose another
   person, and the research measures behind the Dark Triad name are validated only as
   *self-report trait scales in studies*, never as remote assessments
   ([Cognitive Empathy and the Dark Triad review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10670677/)).
   This app provides no tool for labeling someone else and will not build one.
2. **Patterns, not incidents.** Everyone is occasionally selfish, charming, or unfair. What this
   guide catalogs as a *pattern* is trajectory: repetition, escalation, and — most diagnostic of
   all — what happens when you hold a boundary. A single incident is information; a pattern is a
   different thing. Your observation log (`DESIGN-personal-defense-profile.md` §3), not this page,
   decides whether the pattern describes your situation.
3. **If this is your life, not your curiosity: support comes first.** Reading about manipulation
   is not the priority when someone controls your money, movement, contact, or safety. Skip to
   [Support paths](#support-paths). A drill cannot assess danger, and neither can an app; trusted
   humans and support organizations can help, and several of the patterns below are documented
   coercive-control markers, not persuasion techniques
   ([Womens Aid — coercive control](https://womensaid.org.uk/information-support/what-is-domestic-abuse/coercive-control/);
   [National Domestic Violence Hotline — power and control](https://www.thehotline.org/identify-abuse/power-and-control/)).

### What the Dark Triad actually is (and why the name misleads)

Three trait constructs studied in research psychology — **Machiavellianism** (strategic, goal-
focused manipulation; studied since Christie & Geis, 1970, per
[PMC12647074](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12647074/)), **narcissism** (entitlement,
grandiosity), and **psychopathy** (callousness, impulsivity) — often studied together because they
share what the literature calls an *exploitative demeanor*: emotionally manipulative, prone to
callous exploitation, though each trait reaches it through different routes
([Nagler et al. 2014, via the review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10670677/)).
A finding that matters for recognition: high trait scores tend to come with **intact cognitive
empathy** — reading people well — alongside reduced affective empathy, which is exactly the
combination that makes someone good at *finding* and *using* another person's pressure points
([same review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10670677/)).

What that means here: **be suspicious of anyone whose read of you is used to steer you, and
suspicious of your own instinct to diagnose them back.** The name is a research label for
self-reported trait scales in studies (with a meta-analytic link to workplace deviance
[O'Boyle et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025679)); it is not a diagnosis, not a test
result, and not a way to sort the people in your life.

## The pattern catalog

Each pattern gives: what it looks like, why it works, and the boundary move. All of these are
observable. None of them require knowing anything about the person's inner traits — that is the
whole point of behavior recognition.

### 1. Guilt + urgency stacking

**Looks like:** "After everything I've done for you, and it has to be decided *now*."
**Why it works:** guilt supplies moral pressure, urgency removes audit time. Each is weak alone;
stacked, the deadline keeps you from noticing the obligation was manufactured.
**Boundary:** split them explicitly. "I hear that this matters to you. I will decide tomorrow."
A legitimate request survives a day. Manufactured urgency escalates when it meets a pause — which
is itself information ([and the accuracy-prompt logic applied to life:
re-run the check per decision](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9051116/)).

### 2. Manufactured obligation (reciprocity inversion)

**Looks like:** escalating "favors" you did not ask for, each one quietly becoming leverage; the
ledger only ever counts what they did.
**Why it works:** reciprocity is a deep social norm; inversion hijacks it by producing debts you
never contracted.
**Boundary:** name the ledger out loud: "I didn't ask for that, and I'm not obligated." Watch the
reaction: a good-faith person recalibrates; the pattern escalates.

### 3. Enforced secrecy and isolation

**Looks like:** "Keep this between us." "Your friends don't really get you." "I don't like how
your sister talks to you." Gradually, your information flows route through one person.
**Why it works:** manipulation needs unobserved space; other perspectives are the audit.
**Boundary:** secrecy *requested* is a signal *received*. Tell one trusted person. This pattern in
a close relationship is a documented coercive-control marker
([Womens Aid](https://womensaid.org.uk/information-support/what-is-domestic-abuse/coercive-control/))
— treat it as bigger than a technique and read [Support paths](#support-paths).

### 4. Reality-testing erosion (gaslighting)

**Looks like:** "That never happened." "You're remembering it wrong." "You're too sensitive —
that's not what I meant." Over time: you stop trusting your own records and start needing theirs.
**Why it works:** it targets the faculty you'd use to detect it. Your memory becomes negotiable
only if the other party is your only archive.
**Boundary:** externalize the archive. Write things down the day they happen (see
[Documentation](#documentation)). The documented pattern in intimate relationships is well
described by the
[GBV Learning Network gaslighting backgrounder](https://www.gbvlearningnetwork.ca/our-work/backgrounders/gaslighting_in_intimate_relationships/index.html).

### 5. Charm and targeted recognition

**Looks like:** being *seen* — quickly, precisely, more than others see you; early intensity;
mirrored values and interests; "they finally get me."
**Why it works:** recognition is one of the strongest social rewards, and the cognitive-empathy
finding above says exactly who is likeliest to deliver it instrumentally
([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10670677/)). Early-warning resources for
abusive relationships describe the same accelerated-intimacy opening
([recognizing early warning signs](https://newhousekc.org/2026/08/10/recognizing-the-early-warning-signs-of-abuse/)).
**Boundary:** rate the *verification*, not the warmth. Charm is pleasant and unfalsifiable; how
they respond when you verify something they said is falsifiable. Note if recognition is
conditional — warmest when you comply.

### 6. Deny–attack–reverse (the DARVO shape)

**Looks like:** you raise a grievance; it is flatly denied; you are now the aggressor ("how dare
you accuse me"); somehow the conversation ends with you apologizing.
**Why it works:** it converts the accountability conversation into a fight about the accusation,
then about your tone. Named DARVO in the abuse-response literature; the closely related
gaslighting dynamic is documented in the
[GBV backgrounder](https://www.gbvlearningnetwork.ca/our-work/backgrounders/gaslighting_in_intimate_relationships/index.html).
**Boundary:** hold the original question and refuse the frame switch, in writing if needed: "That's
a separate topic. My question was X." One loop of this is conflict; a repeating loop is the
pattern.

### 7. Financial control

**Looks like:** money you earn that you can't access; purchases that require approval; debt used
as leash; "let me handle the finances, you're bad with numbers."
**Why it works:** economic dependence removes the exit option, which makes every other pattern
stickier.
**Boundary:** this is the power-and-control core of documented abuse
([National DV Hotline — power and control](https://www.thehotline.org/identify-abuse/power-and-control/)).
Not a drill target: a support-organization target. See [Support paths](#support-paths).

### 8. Workplace variants

**Looks like:** credit appropriation; boundary-testing that escalates one small transgression at
a time; process weaponized ("I was just following the exception"); colleagues managed against each
other; your doubts about yourself doing the enforcement ("maybe I am too sensitive").
**Why it works:** organizational trust and ambiguity give cover; the meta-analytic link between
these traits and workplace deviance is well documented
([O'Boyle et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0025679); systematic review of Dark Triad traits
and workplace bullying: [PMC12995606](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12995606/)).
**Boundary:** move the pattern from perception to record — dates, decisions, witnesses — and use
the process against the process-abuse: written confirmations ("summarizing what we agreed…").
Documentation beats calibration arguments; see the ACH habit of writing down competing readings of
the same event.

### 9. Instrumental empathy

**Looks like:** accurate guesses about your insecurities, deployed at decision moments: the
perfect flattery for the approval-seeker, the perfect scarcity story for the anxious, the perfect
cause for the idealist.
**Why it works:** being read *well* feels like being understood; the read is the payload
([cognitive-empathy profile](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10670677/)).
**Boundary:** notice *when* the read arrives. Understanding offered at your decision points, in
the direction of their interests, is a move. The Personal Defense Profile's pressure-context
inventory exists precisely so you know which read would work on you before someone tries it.

## What this catalog is not

- **Not a diagnosis.** No pattern, and not even all nine, tells you what someone *is*. The
  research constructs are self-report trait scales; there is no valid remote assessment, and
  guessing one is the exact misuse this guide exists to prevent.
- **Not a sorting tool.** Difficult, selfish, or clashy people are not Dark Triad cases. If you
  find yourself testing everyone you know against this list, that is the guide being misused —
  including on yourself. Scrutiny belongs on *repeated behavior under boundaries*, not on
  personalities.
- **Not a substitute for evidence.** The observation log is the arbiter. If the patterns here do
  not match what you actually logged, the log wins.

## Boundaries

These patterns share one property worth exploiting back: **they are calibrated to compliance, and
they recalibrate when compliance stops.** Three rules of thumb:

1. **State it once, plainly, without persuasion.** "No." "I'm not comfortable with that." "I'll
   decide tomorrow." Over-explaining supplies handles to work on. The Personal Defense Profile's
   countermeasure plans are if-then statements for exactly this: pre-decided, short, deliverable
   under pressure.
2. **The reaction to the boundary is the data.** Respected → recalibrated → no further evidence.
   Pushed, tested, resented, or punished → pattern evidence, and worth logging. This is why the
   observation log's `resisted` field matters: it records what happened when you said no.
3. **Escalation through a pattern is different from friction in an event.** One bad argument is a
   relationship. A repeating deny–attack–reverse loop, or boundaries that consistently cost you,
   is a pattern — and patterns in the coercive-control family are support-organization territory,
   not self-help territory.

## Documentation

**Purpose, in order:** (1) counteracting reality-testing erosion — your own contemporaneous record
is the antidote to "that never happened"; (2) clarifying pattern vs. incident for *yourself*;
(3) something concrete to hand a professional, HR function, or support organization if you later
choose to involve one.

**What to record, the day it happens:**

| Field | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Date and context | when and where, so "you're exaggerating" has an anchor |
| What happened, factually | behavior, not interpretation — "said X, then Y" |
| Quote where possible | exact words beat paraphrase, especially against "I never said that" |
| Who else was present | witnesses, even reluctant ones |
| What you did | your boundary or response — this is the app's `action` field |
| What happened after | did the pattern recalibrate or escalate — the `resisted` field |
| Effect on you | honest, because your state is part of the record too |

This maps one-to-one onto the Personal Defense Profile's observation record (spec §3: tactic,
contextTag, initialReaction, action, outcome, confidence), so logging inside the app and logging
for yourself are the same act.

**Safety caveat:** if the person has access to your devices, accounts, or files, a record they can
find is not a safe record. If device monitoring or tech abuse is part of your situation, ask a
support organization about tech-safety practices before building any documentation
([National DV Hotline](https://www.thehotline.org/identify-abuse/power-and-control/) is a starting
point), and weigh what a discovered document might cost you. Documentation serves safety; it must
never be allowed to endanger the person it is meant to protect.

## Support paths

- **One trusted person, named in advance.** Isolation tactics work against people with no
  outside witness; the cheapest structural defense is an outside witness chosen before you need
  one. This is the countermeasure the app itself gives for the isolation watchpoint — and it is
  deliberately a human, not a drill.
- **Support organizations.** If any of this is happening inside a close relationship:
  [Womens Aid — coercive control](https://womensaid.org.uk/information-support/what-is-domestic-abuse/coercive-control/)
  (UK), [National Domestic Violence Hotline](https://www.thehotline.org/identify-abuse/power-and-control/)
  (US) — or your local equivalent; most regions have one, and searching "domestic abuse support"
  plus your country is a safe first query even on a monitored device.
- **Workplace:** HR, employee assistance programs, or a union representative — with
  documentation, per the section above, because pattern-vs-incident is exactly the distinction
  those processes are bad at deciding from memory.
- **Emergency services** where there is immediate danger. An app is never the right first call
  for immediate danger, and this one will never pretend otherwise.
- **Inside this app:** the Personal Defense Profile's support card (spec §8,
  `data/profile-guides.json`) appears when an observation carries coercive-control markers. It
  offers this page's human paths. It performs **no danger assessment** — a static, deliberate
  boundary, because a local app with no context must not grade anyone's safety.

## The honest bottom line

The Dark Triad name makes this sound like a way to spot bad people. It is the opposite: a catalog
of *moves*, so that the question "what kind of person is this?" — unanswerable, trap-laden — can
be replaced by the question "what happened when I held a boundary?" — observable, recordable,
actionable. The trait research can tell you which strategies exploit which pressure points
([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10670677/)); only your own log can tell you
whether anyone is using them on you; and only humans and support organizations can help when the
answer is yes.
